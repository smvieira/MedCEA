Research sandbox

The research sandbox is where control models and recipes are tried on a simulated chamber before anyone proposes them to the platform. It is its own application beside the operational services. It runs production's own controllers and loop rules, reads production's own catalogue and board definitions, and never writes to the operational services. This chapter says what the sandbox is and is not, the four interfaces through which it touches the rest of MedCEA, what Phase 1 built (the chamber model, the simulation harness, the metrics and campaigns), how to run a controller comparison campaign, what its manifest proves, how Phase 2a separates the research zone and fills the research database with snapshots (the zone, the database, the held-out reservations, the nightly export), how Phase 2b moves the AI Workbench into that zone and lets its notebooks read the snapshots, and what is not built yet.

What the sandbox is, and what it is not

The reference architecture (version 2, section 4) places a Research Sandbox beside the Compliance Plane. When it is complete it holds:

  • a copy of operational data (measurements, runs, recipes), taken as snapshots, in which no person appears (not even under a pseudonym: The nightly export);
  • the digital twins of the chamber and of the crop, and a closed-loop simulation harness that runs a controller against a twin;
  • an optimisation engine that searches for better parameter sets and recipes;
  • an agent harness: AI agents that run campaigns and write up what they find, whose output is only ever proposed;
  • a candidate registry, from which a candidate reaches the platform only through a gateway, as a draft that qa approves like any other;
  • a validation vault: data kept back for validation, which no developer and no agent can read.

It is not:

  • A way to change a chamber. Nothing the sandbox produces drives equipment. A parameter set or recipe from it runs on a chamber only after it was written into the Configuration UI as a draft and approved by a qa person who did not author it (Approvals of configuration).
  • A second operational database. It never connects to the platform's database (the coupling contract). What it knows about real runs it learns from snapshots.
  • A regulated record. A campaign's results are evidence for a proposal, attached to it. The regulated records stay where they are: the approval, the policy history and the run records of the platform.
  • A place for production code. The controllers it runs are production's, loaded from the repository; a change to a controller is made in schd/cea/algorithms/ and deployed to the scheduler the usual way.

The owner decided on 16 November 2026 to build the whole sandbox, in eight phases; to give each agent session the choice of a hosted model (Claude) or a local model on the host's CPUs; to write the agent loop ourselves, minimal and in Python; and to start with controller campaigns for the control_scientist: comparing and tuning on/off (If-Then-Else), PID, FLC, MPC and Q-learning on the chamber model, to prepare Experiment 1 (May 2027). The decisions are in the decision record.

The chamber model

The chamber model (research/twin_plant/chamber.py, standard library only) is one 60 × 60 × 60 cm chamber (0.216 m³) described by four quantities: air temperature, substrate temperature (published as ground_temperature), water vapour in the air (from which relative humidity follows) and CO2. They change through simple balances, advanced in steps of at most one second:

  • Air temperature: heat through the walls and with the air exchanged with the room, to and from the substrate, from the heater, the fans, the lamps and the fungi, minus what the cooler pumps out and the latent heat of the humidifier's mist. With the fans off and the door shut its time constant is 146 minutes by construction.
  • Substrate temperature: to and from the air (four times faster while the air flow fan runs), to the room through the floor, and from the substrate heater.
  • Humidity: water from the humidifier and the fungi, and the air exchange with the room; capped at saturation.
  • CO2: the fungi's respiration and the air exchange with the room. The CO2 fan exchanges much more air than the leak.

The fungi's respiration, water and heat follow the substrate temperature with a Q10 of 2. The relays are the CONTROL board's (air_heater, air_cooler, air_humidifier, air_flow_fan, co2_fan, co2_steriliser, substrate_heater and the red, blue and UV lamps); each relay's energy is its power times its time on.

Every number below is an assumption until Phase 3. Only the volume is a hardware fact, and the 146-minute time constant an estimate from the chamber's own data. The rest are assumed nameplates and textbook figures, to be calibrated against the chamber's recorded measurements once snapshots exist. Results from Phase 1 compare controllers with one another on the same model; they do not predict a real chamber's numbers.

The sensors (research/twin_plant/sensors.py) turn the model's true values into what a SENSE board would publish: an SHT45 (air temperature and humidity), an SCD30 (CO2, air temperature and humidity) and a DS18B20 on the substrate. A datasheet accuracy of ±a is taken as two standard deviations of independent noise per message (an assumption: real sensors also drift, which a scenario can add as a constant bias); the SCD30's CO2 noise has its fixed part (30 ppm) and its 3 % part. Values are quantised as the sensor and the board do (two decimals; 0.0625 °C for the DS18B20). Each sensor publishes every 30 seconds (the SENSE board's default interval), offset from the others, and a scenario may add latency and lost messages. All of it is drawn from random number streams seeded by the scenario's seed, one per sensor, so the same seed gives the same readings. The SEN0562 light sensor is not modelled: light intensity is not a quantity a loop controls yet, and the chamber model has no light balance (Not built yet). A starting value a scenario leaves out is the room's, the substrate's included.

The simulation harness

The harness (research/harness/) runs one scenario as production would run it: the runner's 10-second control tick, re-expressed with the same functions of schd/cea/loop_resolver.py in the same order. At each tick it takes the newest readings, decides whether the light is on, and for each active loop resolves its input (the reference sensor, its fallback, or the default map's rule), asks the controller, applies the loop's command map (CO2: DOWN means fans on) and publishes the command. The board model applies the commands in publishing order with the template's semantics, so the shared air_flow_fan ends a tick as the last command left it, as on the real board. At each recipe cycle's end the controllers restart and keep their state as production's runner does: new controller instances and a new alarm tracker, and readings that reached the old cycle's subscriber are not carried into the new one. A loop whose controller raises an error is counted and the run marked invalid, while the other loops and the light go on, as in production. A loop whose input is something the chamber model does not compute, or that no configured sensor publishes, is refused before the run.

A scenario (YAML, in research/harness/scenarios/) names a recipe type, one recipe step's setpoints, the light, the starting state, the room, disturbances (a door opening, extra heat, the room changing) and, optionally, the zone's control loop rows as onboarding saves them. Its setpoints are checked by the database's own rules for a recipe step (the trigger recipe_line_setpoints_trg, with the same messages), and its loops by loop_resolver's, so a scenario the platform would refuse is refused here too. Two scenarios ship: smoke_air_temperature (one hour, starting below the air temperature band) and fungi_pleurotus_fruiting (48 hours of the Pleurotus ostreatus fruiting step in a warm room, with a door opening and the room warming).

Production's code, loaded by path. The harness loads schd/cea/algorithms/ and loop_resolver.py from the repository by their paths, never through the scheduler's package, which would pull in the database and broker libraries. It replaces two things for the length of a run: PID's clock (below) and Q-learning's random source, both seeded by the scenario. It refuses a controller code that is not in the list of codes, because production's factory silently runs on/off control for an unknown code, and a parameter set the controller's schema refuses.

Fallbacks are counted, and make a run invalid

FLC needs scikit-fuzzy and MPC needs GEKKO. Without the library, or when the fuzzy computation or the solver fails, each falls back to on/off control. Since 17 November 2026 both count it (per controller, with the reason) and write one line the first time, then at most one an hour: WARNING flc fallback: … or WARNING mpc fallback: …. The harness counts per loop how many ticks the controller answered with its library, how many by the fallback, and how many it was not asked at all (no data). A run in which an FLC or MPC controller fell back even once is marked invalid ("valid": false, with the reason), because its numbers would be on/off control's under another name. The host's own python3 has neither library, so a campaign run with it marks every FLC and MPC run invalid; build the virtual environment first (below).

PID depends on the clock: a property of production PID

Production's PID turns its output into a duty cycle within a repeating window of cycle_time seconds (default 10): it commands UP or DOWN while now % cycle_time is below |output| × cycle_time, else OFF. now is the host's monotonic clock, whose origin is arbitrary. With the default window equal to the 10-second tick, now % cycle_time is nearly the same at every tick, so whether PID ever fires depends on where in the window the ticks happen to fall: at a phase near 0 it fires whenever its output is not zero (like on/off control), at a phase near 9.99 seconds only when its output is above 0.999. Which of these a real run gets is decided by when the scheduler pod started. The harness therefore sets the clock's origin per run (clock_offsets_s) and campaigns sweep it; tick jitter (jitter_s: production waits 10 seconds plus the time one pass takes) moves the phase slowly. This is how production PID behaves today, not a defect of the harness. A different cycle_time is a parameter set like any other, proposed and approved through the Configuration UI.

Metrics

Each run is scored on the model's true values (never a sensor reading), sampled once per tick, from the scenario's metrics_from_h on (its settling time). Per loop, with lower limit L, upper limit U, band width W = U − L, midpoint M = (L + U) / 2 and the N samples xi:

MetricFormulaReads as
rmsd_mid√( (1/N) Σ (xi − M)² )Distance from the middle of the band, in the quantity's unit. Lower is closer to the middle.
out_of_band(1/N) · #{ i : xi < L or xi > U }Share of the time outside the band.
mean_abs_error(1/N) Σ max(L − xi, 0, xi − U)How far outside the band, on average (0 inside it).
boundary_hugging#{ in band and within 0.1 W of an edge } / #{ in band }Share of the in-band time spent next to an edge. On/off control parks the value near an edge and scores high. Empty when no sample is in band.
command_changesnumber of ticks whose published command differs from the loop's previous oneHow restless the loop is.

Per relay: switches, the number of ticks at which the relay's state differs from the previous tick (the shared air flow fan counts once per tick), and energy_wh, its power times its time on over the whole run, settling included; energy_total_wh is their sum. The formulas are version 1 (metrics_version in every result); any change to a formula raises the version, and campaigns are compared only within one version.

Running a campaign

Phase 1 runs on the platform host, in the repository, as the account that holds the checkout. It reads nothing from the cluster and writes only its output folder. The harness itself needs only Python and PyYAML; FLC and MPC need the virtual environment research/.venv, which research/lib-venv.sh builds from research/requirements.txt: numpy, scipy, scikit-fuzzy, GEKKO, networkx, packaging (which scikit-fuzzy needs but does not declare) and PyYAML, each pinned by version and sha256 and installed binary only, so a changed file on PyPI is refused. It needs python3-venv and access to PyPI the first time; a second run installs nothing.

source research/lib-venv.sh && ensure_research_venv
research/.venv/bin/python -m research.harness.campaign SPEC.yaml [--out DIR] [--jobs N] [--max-cpu-hours H] [--allow-dirty]

Two specs ship in research/harness/campaigns/:

SpecWhat it runsCommand
smoke.yamlThe five controllers, each with a two-value grid of one parameter, on the one-hour smoke scenario; two seeds, clock offsets 0 and 9.99 s: 36 runs, a few CPU-minutes.research/.venv/bin/python -m research.harness.campaign research/harness/campaigns/smoke.yaml
fruiting_compare.yamlThe five controllers at their default parameters on the 48-hour Pleurotus fruiting scenario; three seeds, three clock offsets: 45 runs. Neither command needs --allow-dirty on a committed checkout. Add it only to try uncommitted changes to the contract sources or to research/; the manifest then lists the changed files, and the result cannot be reproduced from a commit.

A campaign spec lists scenarios (files, relative to the spec, or written inline), controllers ({code, params, params_grid}; a grid is expanded to every combination), seeds, clock offsets and jitter. Every combination is one run. The output folder (default research/runs/<spec name>) gets results.jsonl, one line per run in a fixed order, and manifest.json. The command prints one line, for example "campaign: 30 runs (0 invalid), results in …, digest 6ea56c3f…".

Before it runs anything the campaign refuses (exit 2, nothing written):

  • an unknown controller code or a parameter outside the controller's schema;
  • a scenario the catalogue or the loop rules would refuse;
  • a repository whose contract sources (schd/cea/, the catalogue, the CONTROL template) or the sandbox's own code (research/, but its virtual environment and research/runs/) differ from the committed version, unless --allow-dirty, which records the changed files in the manifest;
  • a campaign whose estimated CPU time is over its cap. One short calibration run per controller measures CPU seconds per simulated hour (GEKKO's solver included); the estimate is that rate times every run's length. The default cap is 4 CPU-hours.

The runs are spread over at most the CPUs this process may use (the cgroup's quota, else half the host's CPUs), never more than --jobs. The result file does not depend on how many processes ran it. The exercise is E39 Run a controller comparison campaign.

What the manifest proves

manifest.json names everything a result depends on:

  • code: the commit, whether the contract sources or research/ were dirty and which files, the git blob hash of every file loaded (each controller module, loop_resolver.py, params.py, the catalogue, the CONTROL template, the database migration file that holds the setpoint rules) and of the sandbox's own code, scenarios, specs and pins, the coupling contract's version (contract_version), the library versions, whether FLC and MPC had their libraries, and the Python version;
  • spec_digest: the sha256 of the spec with every scenario file inlined and every default filled in;
  • the seeds, clock offsets, jitter, metrics version, number of runs and of invalid runs, and the CPU estimate and cap;
  • result_digest: the sha256 of results.jsonl.

The same spec on the same commit, with the same library versions, gives the same result_digest, whoever runs it and with however many processes. That is what makes a campaign evidence: anyone can rerun it from the manifest and compare one hash. A different digest means something listed in the manifest differs. The manifest proves which code and inputs produced the numbers; it does not prove that the chamber model matches a real chamber (Phase 3), and on its own it is not a record of who ran the campaign.

Controller libraries in production

Building Phase 1 found two defects in production's controllers, fixed with the owner's approval on 17 November 2026 (decision record):

  • MPC's command was inverted. Below its band it answered DOWN (cool) and above it UP (heat). No run ever used MPC, because the scheduler pod did not have GEKKO, so MPC always ran as on/off control. The sign is fixed.
  • FLC fell back to on/off control even with scikit-fuzzy installed, because scikit-fuzzy 0.5.0 needs packaging without declaring it.

The scheduler pod now installs GEKKO, scikit-fuzzy and their dependencies, pinned by version and checksum (apps/scheduler/requirements-controllers.txt), and the health check Controller libraries warns when they are missing (Control loops; exercise E40). The libraries are deployed with ./apps/scheduler/deploy.sh while no run is active. Choosing MPC or FLC for a unit, or a new parameter set for either, is a configuration change approved by qa as before.

The research database

./apps/research/deploy.sh (./deploy-apps.sh --all runs it; ./medcea.sh → Applications → Install the research database) installs the research sandbox's own PostgreSQL, Helm release research-postgresql (Bitnami chart 18.4.0, the platform's version) in the research namespace, at the low priority, with its volume data-research-postgresql-0 (RESEARCH_DB_STORAGE, default 10Gi). It never touches the platform's database server. It skips with one line and exit 0 while RESEARCH_ENABLED is not true, while the applied access policy has no research zone, while no policy version is applied, and while the research namespace does not exist yet; a research database already installed is left running by a skip.

The chart's own NetworkPolicy is switched off: it would have opened port 5432 to the whole cluster. After the install the deploy stops if any NetworkPolicy in the research namespace lacks the label medcea/rs-netpol=true, naming it.

The schemas: lake (the snapshots), vault (the held-out reservations), and sim, agent_trace and registry, created empty for later phases. The lake and the vault are append-only: triggers refuse UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE on every lake table and on vault.reservation ("… is append-only: … refused"), for rs_owner too, and they are ENABLE ALWAYS, so a replication session does not bypass them. Outside the platform's access policy. The research database is not in the policy matrix and nobody signs in to it as a person. Its grants are written by hand in apps/research/db/grants.sql, and every deploy ends by checking them from the catalog (apps/research/db/assert_grants.sql): rs_export_writer holds exactly what the table above says and owns nothing, rs_backup is a member of pg_read_all_data only, PUBLIC holds nothing, and rs_owner cannot log in and owns everything. Any difference stops the deploy and lists it. The passwords (RESEARCH_DB_PASS for the server's superuser, RS_EXPORT_WRITER_DB_PASS, RS_BACKUP_DB_PASS) are generated by infra/configure.sh, never asked or shown, and reach PostgreSQL only as SCRAM verifiers.

Backup. The nightly backup dumps medcea_research from the research server as rs_backup, next to the platform's databases, whenever Secret research-backup exists and the database answers; without it its MANIFEST says the database was skipped, and a research server that does not answer is no failure of the platform's backup. A backup deployed from this code needs no redeploy when research is switched on later (it always knows the research server's address); the enable recipe runs ./apps/backup/deploy.sh anyway.

Removing it. ./apps/research/uninstall.sh removes the two Secrets and the release and keeps the volume, so a later deploy finds the same snapshots and reservations. ./apps/research/uninstall.sh --purge-data also deletes the volume: every snapshot and every reservation is gone. ./deploy-apps.sh --uninstall --purge-data never purges it; that is this script's own flag.

Held-out reservations

A reservation keeps one unit's measurements, or one zone's, with a measurement time in [from, to) out of every snapshot from then on, so a model can later be judged on data it never saw (the final validation, RS.8). Only a qa person reserves, through a command on the host:

./apps/research/reserve.sh add --by <your qa username> --unit <UNIT> [--zone <ZONE>] --from <TIME> --to <TIME> --reason "<why, 10+ characters>"
./apps/research/reserve.sh list
  • --by names the qa person making it, as their account in the platform's realm: it must exist, be enabled and hold the role qa, else "--by … does not hold the realm role qa: only qa reserves held-out data." Its username and account id are recorded with the reason.
  • TIME is ISO 8601, such as 2026-12-01 or 2026-12-01T06:00, in UTC unless it carries an offset. Without --zone every zone of the unit is held out.
  • add shows what it will hold out, says "This cannot be changed or removed afterwards." and asks for a typed yes; it takes the deploy lock. On success: "Reservation #N made: unit … [from, to) is held out of every snapshot from now on". list is read-only: "No reservations: nothing is held out of the research lake.", or a table ID, UNIT, ZONE, FROM, TO, BY, MADE, REASON (ZONE * = every zone).
  • Before the first export that would carry the data. The research database refuses a range whose measurements are already in the lake: "reserve: unit … is already exported: it is no longer held out". It also refuses a range that ends more than a year from now and a reason shorter than 10 characters. A reservation is never changed or removed.

A reservation is not an account decision and is not on the policy history: it is a row of vault.reservation in the research database (who, when, why), written as rs_owner through vault.reserve, outside the policy matrix. In ./medcea.sh: Applications → Reserve a held-out range (qa), and List the held-out reservations. The snapshot export holds a reservation out from the moment it is made; live reads by the scientists end only at the Phase 2b switchover (decision record, 17 November 2026). From Phase 2b a notebook sees which ranges are held out (unit, zone, from, to), never who made a reservation or why (Reading snapshots from a notebook).

The nightly export

./apps/research-export/deploy.sh (./medcea.sh → Applications → Install the nightly research export) installs CronJob research-export in the platform namespace: daily at 03:00 UTC, after the nightly backup, one run at a time, in the image the research database runs (so its psql matches), with no service account token and a read-only root file system. It skips like the research database, and also until that database is installed and while IAM_DB_SERVICE_ROLES is not true; it never falls back to the platform's superuser. One run:

  1. Asks the research database for the latest cut (lake.latest_cut(), none before the first snapshot).
  2. Takes the new cut from the operational database: its clock minus two minutes, so a reading whose transaction started before the cut has committed. It is refused when the cut is not after the latest one.
  3. In one read-only, repeatable-read transaction on the operational database, as svc_research_export, reads every medcea.research_*() function: the measurements ingested in (previous cut, cut], the small sets whole.
  4. In one transaction on the research database, as rs_export_writer, stages the rows and calls lake.close_snapshot, which drops every row inside a reservation, digests the rest, records the snapshot and moves the rows into the lake. Any error rolls back the whole of it: nothing is half written.
  5. Prints "research-export: snapshot N cut … measurements M", where M counts the rows read, before the reservations removed theirs.

Run one at once with microk8s kubectl -n medcea create job --from=cronjob/research-export research-export-now-1; ./apps/research-export/uninstall.sh removes the CronJob, and the snapshots stay.

Snapshots and their digests

The lake is append-only and a snapshot is a high-water mark (owner decision S). Each export adds one row to lake.snapshot: snapshot_id, cut_at (the cut), previous_cut_at, created_at, contract_version, rows and digests. The measurements accumulate, each snapshot adding those ingested since the previous cut; the small sets (units, zones, recipes, recipe lines, runs, cycles, control loops, parameter sets, catalogue) are copied whole into every snapshot, each row tagged with its snapshot_id. close_snapshot refuses a contract version it does not know, a previous cut that is not the latest snapshot's (another export ran in between), a cut not after it, and a staged measurement outside (previous cut, cut].

  • rows: per set, its row count, plus held_out, the staged measurements a reservation kept out.
  • digests: per set, a sha256. For a small set it is the sha256 of its rows' text forms (under time zone UTC and ISO dates, sorted bytewise) joined with newlines.
  • For the measurements it is computed per hour of ingest time, so no step holds more than an hour of rows: for each hour that has rows, oldest first, the line <hour as YYYY-MM-DDTHH:00:00Z>|<rows in that hour>|<sha256 of that hour's sorted row texts joined with newlines>; the digest is the sha256 of those lines joined with newlines. A set with no rows digests the empty string.

The health check Research export (rsexport) reads the newest snapshot read-only: OK while it is at most 26 hours old (for example "newest snapshot 1 h old (snapshot 12, no reserved rows in it);2 reservation(s)"), WARN beyond, FAIL beyond 72 hours, as for backups; FAIL when rows of it fall in a held-out reservation ("… held-out data reached the lake"); WARN while there is no reservation ("no reservation yet: nothing is held out for validation …") and when something cannot be read; SKIP without the CronJob and, with no snapshot yet, during the CronJob's first 26 hours. With Research network policies it brings the health check from 66 to 68 checks.

The order to switch it on, once the skeleton version with the research zone is applied, is what the RESEARCH_ENABLED enable recipe of ./medcea.sh runs: set RESEARCH_ENABLED=true, ./deploy-infra.sh (the namespace and its policies), ./apps/database/deploy.sh (the export's login and Secret db-svc-research-export), ./apps/research/deploy.sh, ./apps/backup/deploy.sh, ./apps/research-export/deploy.sh. Then a qa person reserves the held-out ranges before the first export, and the first export is started by hand or waits for 03:00 UTC (exercise E42). The rollback recipe sets it false and uninstalls the export, then the research database, keeping its volume.

Reading snapshots from a notebook (Phase 2b)

The snapshot reader lake-api (./apps/lake-api/deploy.sh, run by ./apps/research/deploy.sh as its last step) is a small read-only web service in the research namespace in front of the research database. It answers only from read functions in the schema lake_api, which return the lake's rows and nothing else, and it holds no identity: by the owner's decision (O2 of 19 November 2026) the network decides who reaches it, notebooks and the host, and the database decides what it answers. Its database login, rs_lake_api, may only execute those functions, in read-only transactions of at most 30 seconds, with at most 10 connections. Nothing a person typed and no person is in the lake (The nightly export), so nothing personal can be read through it.
