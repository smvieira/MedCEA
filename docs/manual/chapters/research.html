Research sandbox

The research sandbox is where control models and recipes are tried on a simulated chamber before anyone proposes them to the platform. It is its own application beside the operational services. It runs production's own controllers and loop rules, reads production's own catalogue and board definitions, and never writes to the operational services. This chapter says what the sandbox is and is not, the four interfaces through which it touches the rest of MedCEA, what Phase 1 built (the chamber model, the simulation harness, the metrics and campaigns), how to run a controller comparison campaign, what its manifest proves, how Phase 2a separates the research zone and fills the research database with snapshots (the zone, the database, the held-out reservations, the nightly export), how Phase 2b moves the AI Workbench into that zone and lets its notebooks read the snapshots, how Phase 3 calibrates the sensor model from a snapshot and runs the comparisons again on it (and why every plant constant is still an assumption), the step-test protocol that will let the plant be fitted, and what is not built yet.

What the sandbox is, and what it is not

The reference architecture (version 2, section 4) places a Research Sandbox beside the Compliance Plane. When it is complete it holds:

  • a copy of operational data (measurements, runs, recipes), taken as snapshots, in which no person appears (not even under a pseudonym: The nightly export);
  • the digital twins of the chamber and of the crop, and a closed-loop simulation harness that runs a controller against a twin;
  • an optimisation engine that searches for better parameter sets and recipes;
  • an agent harness: AI agents that run campaigns and write up what they find, whose output is only ever proposed;
  • a candidate registry, from which a candidate reaches the platform only through a gateway, as a draft that qa approves like any other;
  • a validation vault: data kept back for validation, which no developer and no agent can read.

It is not:

  • A way to change a chamber. Nothing the sandbox produces drives equipment. A parameter set or recipe from it runs on a chamber only after it was written into the Configuration UI as a draft and approved by a qa person who did not author it (Approvals of configuration).
  • A second operational database. It never connects to the platform's database (the coupling contract). What it knows about real runs it learns from snapshots.
  • A regulated record. A campaign's results are evidence for a proposal, attached to it. The regulated records stay where they are: the approval, the policy history and the run records of the platform.
  • A place for production code. The controllers it runs are production's, loaded from the repository; a change to a controller is made in schd/cea/algorithms/ and deployed to the scheduler the usual way.

The owner decided on 16 November 2026 to build the whole sandbox, in eight phases; to give each agent session the choice of a hosted model (Claude) or a local model on the host's CPUs; to write the agent loop ourselves, minimal and in Python; and to start with controller campaigns for the control_scientist: comparing and tuning on/off (If-Then-Else), PID, FLC, MPC and Q-learning on the chamber model, to prepare Experiment 1 (May 2027). The decisions are in the decision record.

The chamber model

The chamber model (research/twin_plant/chamber.py, standard library only) is one 60 × 60 × 60 cm chamber (0.216 m³) described by four quantities: air temperature, substrate temperature (published as ground_temperature), water vapour in the air (from which relative humidity follows) and CO2. They change through simple balances, advanced in steps of at most one second:

  • Air temperature: heat through the walls and with the air exchanged with the room, to and from the substrate, from the heater, the fans, the lamps and the fungi, minus what the cooler pumps out and the latent heat of the humidifier's mist. With the fans off and the door shut its time constant is 146 minutes by construction.
  • Substrate temperature: to and from the air (four times faster while the air flow fan runs), to the room through the floor, and from the substrate heater.
  • Humidity: water from the humidifier and the fungi, and the air exchange with the room; capped at saturation.
  • CO2: the fungi's respiration and the air exchange with the room. The CO2 fan exchanges much more air than the leak.

The fungi's respiration, water and heat follow the substrate temperature with a Q10 of 2. The relays are the CONTROL board's (air_heater, air_cooler, air_humidifier, air_flow_fan, co2_fan, co2_steriliser, substrate_heater and the red, blue and UV lamps); each relay's energy is its power times its time on.

The sensor model is calibrated from snapshot 1 (lineage 096015bc225c…); every plant constant below is still an assumption, which the fit refuses until step-test data exists. Only the volume is a hardware fact, and the 146-minute time constant an estimate from the chamber's own data. No snapshot so far shows an actuator switching or the room's conditions, so nothing in the table can be fitted yet (Calibrating the sensor model from a snapshot, The step-test protocol). Results compare controllers with one another on the same model; they do not predict a real chamber's numbers.

The sensors (research/twin_plant/sensors.py) turn the model's true values into what a SENSE board would publish: an SHT45 (air temperature and humidity), an SCD30 (CO2, air temperature and humidity) and a DS18B20 on the substrate. A datasheet accuracy of ±a is taken as two standard deviations of independent noise per message (an assumption: real sensors also drift, which a scenario can add as a constant bias); the SCD30's CO2 noise has its fixed part (30 ppm) and its 3 % part. Values are quantised as the sensor and the board do (two decimals; 0.0625 °C for the DS18B20). Each sensor publishes every 30 seconds (the SENSE board's default interval), offset from the others, and a scenario may add latency and lost messages. All of it is drawn from random number streams seeded by the scenario's seed, one per sensor, so the same seed gives the same readings. The SEN0562 light sensor is not modelled: light intensity is not a quantity a loop controls yet, and the chamber model has no light balance (Not built yet). A starting value a scenario leaves out is the room's, the substrate's included.

The simulation harness

The harness (research/harness/) runs one scenario as production would run it: the runner's 10-second control tick, re-expressed with the same functions of schd/cea/loop_resolver.py in the same order. At each tick it takes the newest readings, decides whether the light is on, and for each active loop resolves its input (the reference sensor, its fallback, or the default map's rule), asks the controller, applies the loop's command map (CO2: DOWN means fans on) and publishes the command. The board model applies the commands in publishing order with the template's semantics, so the shared air_flow_fan ends a tick as the last command left it, as on the real board. At each recipe cycle's end the controllers restart and keep their state as production's runner does: new controller instances and a new alarm tracker, and readings that reached the old cycle's subscriber are not carried into the new one. A loop whose controller raises an error is counted and the run marked invalid, while the other loops and the light go on, as in production. A loop whose input is something the chamber model does not compute, or that no configured sensor publishes, is refused before the run.

A scenario (YAML, in research/harness/scenarios/) names a recipe type, one recipe step's setpoints, the light, the starting state, the room, disturbances (a door opening, extra heat, the room changing) and, optionally, the zone's control loop rows as onboarding saves them. Its setpoints are checked by the database's own rules for a recipe step (the trigger recipe_line_setpoints_trg, with the same messages), and its loops by loop_resolver's, so a scenario the platform would refuse is refused here too. Two scenarios ship: smoke_air_temperature (one hour, starting below the air temperature band) and fungi_pleurotus_fruiting (48 hours of the Pleurotus ostreatus fruiting step in a warm room, with a door opening and the room warming).

Production's code, loaded by path. The harness loads schd/cea/algorithms/ and loop_resolver.py from the repository by their paths, never through the scheduler's package, which would pull in the database and broker libraries. It replaces two things for the length of a run: PID's clock (below) and Q-learning's random source, both seeded by the scenario. It refuses a controller code that is not in the list of codes, because production's factory silently runs on/off control for an unknown code, and a parameter set the controller's schema refuses.

Fallbacks are counted, and make a run invalid

FLC needs scikit-fuzzy and MPC needs GEKKO. Without the library, or when the fuzzy computation or the solver fails, each falls back to on/off control. Since 17 November 2026 both count it (per controller, with the reason) and write one line the first time, then at most one an hour: WARNING flc fallback: … or WARNING mpc fallback: …. The harness counts per loop how many ticks the controller answered with its library, how many by the fallback, and how many it was not asked at all (no data). A run in which an FLC or MPC controller fell back even once is marked invalid ("valid": false, with the reason), because its numbers would be on/off control's under another name. The host's own python3 has neither library, so a campaign run with it marks every FLC and MPC run invalid; build the virtual environment first (below).

PID depends on the clock: a property of production PID

Production's PID turns its output into a duty cycle within a repeating window of cycle_time seconds (default 10): it commands UP or DOWN while now % cycle_time is below |output| × cycle_time, else OFF. now is the host's monotonic clock, whose origin is arbitrary. With the default window equal to the 10-second tick, now % cycle_time is nearly the same at every tick, so whether PID ever fires depends on where in the window the ticks happen to fall: at a phase near 0 it fires whenever its output is not zero (like on/off control), at a phase near 9.99 seconds only when its output is above 0.999. Which of these a real run gets is decided by when the scheduler pod started. The harness therefore sets the clock's origin per run (clock_offsets_s) and campaigns sweep it; tick jitter (jitter_s: production waits 10 seconds plus the time one pass takes) moves the phase slowly. This is how production PID behaves today, not a defect of the harness. A different cycle_time is a parameter set like any other, proposed and approved through the Configuration UI.

Each run is scored on the model's true values (never a sensor reading), sampled once per tick, from the scenario's metrics_from_h on (its settling time). Per loop, with lower limit L, upper limit U, band width W = U − L, midpoint M = (L + U) / 2 and the N samples xi:

Running a campaign

Phase 1 runs on the platform host, in the repository, as the account that holds the checkout. It reads nothing from the cluster and writes only its output folder. The harness itself needs only Python and PyYAML; FLC and MPC need the virtual environment research/.venv, which research/lib-venv.sh builds from research/requirements.txt: numpy, scipy, scikit-fuzzy, GEKKO, networkx, packaging (which scikit-fuzzy needs but does not declare) and PyYAML, each pinned by version and sha256 and installed binary only, so a changed file on PyPI is refused. It needs python3-venv and access to PyPI the first time; a second run installs nothing.

A campaign spec lists scenarios (files, relative to the spec, or written inline), controllers ({code, params, params_grid}; a grid is expanded to every combination), seeds, clock offsets and jitter. Every combination is one run. The output folder (default research/runs/<spec name>) gets results.jsonl, one line per run in a fixed order, and manifest.json. The command prints one line, for example "campaign: 30 runs (0 invalid), results in …, digest 6ea56c3f…".

Before it runs anything the campaign refuses (exit 2, nothing written):

  • an unknown controller code or a parameter outside the controller's schema;
  • a scenario the catalogue or the loop rules would refuse;
  • a repository whose contract sources (schd/cea/, the catalogue, the CONTROL template) or the sandbox's own code (research/, but its virtual environment and research/runs/) differ from the committed version, unless --allow-dirty, which records the changed files in the manifest;
  • a campaign whose estimated CPU time is over its cap. One short calibration run per controller measures CPU seconds per simulated hour (GEKKO's solver included); the estimate is that rate times every run's length. The default cap is 4 CPU-hours.

The runs are spread over at most the CPUs this process may use (the cgroup's quota, else half the host's CPUs), never more than --jobs. The result file does not depend on how many processes ran it. The exercise is E39 Run a controller comparison campaign.

What the manifest proves

manifest.json names everything a result depends on:

  • code: the commit, whether the contract sources or research/ were dirty and which files, the git blob hash of every file loaded (each controller module, loop_resolver.py, params.py, the catalogue, the CONTROL template, the database migration file that holds the setpoint rules) and of the sandbox's own code, scenarios, specs and pins, the coupling contract's version (contract_version), the library versions, whether FLC and MPC had their libraries, and the Python version;
  • spec_digest: the sha256 of the spec with every scenario file inlined and every default filled in;
  • the seeds, clock offsets, jitter, metrics version, number of runs and of invalid runs, and the CPU estimate and cap;
  • result_digest: the sha256 of results.jsonl;
  • from Phase 3, code.sensor_model_version (2: the sensor model gained the SCD30's self-heating and a per-run absolute error, both off unless a calibration or scenario sets them), contract_version 2 (the reviewed contract also names the lake reader's functions; the lake's own export contract is still 1), and, only when a scenario pins a calibration or a recipe line, provenance: per scenario, the calibration's path, sha256, name, snapshot, lineage, dataset digest and code, every value applied and every value not applied with the reason, the humidity range it was fitted on and the scenario's reason for going beyond it, and the recipe line's extract, snapshot, lineage, digests, recipe and step and any photoperiod it replaced.

Beside manifest.json the campaign writes spec.json: the resolved spec, every scenario and pin inlined, exactly the bytes spec_digest hashes, so the digest can be checked by hand.

The same spec on the same commit, with the same library versions, gives the same result_digest, whoever runs it and with however many processes. That is what makes a campaign evidence: anyone can rerun it from the manifest and compare one hash. A different digest means something listed in the manifest differs. The manifest proves which code and inputs produced the numbers; it does not prove that the chamber model matches a real chamber (Phase 3), and on its own it is not a record of who ran the campaign. A calibrated campaign's manifest proves which snapshot the sensor model was fitted to; it still says nothing about the chamber's plant constants, which are assumptions (Calibrating the sensor model from a snapshot).

Controller libraries in production

Building Phase 1 found two defects in production's controllers, fixed with the owner's approval on 17 November 2026 (decision record):

  • MPC's command was inverted. Below its band it answered DOWN (cool) and above it UP (heat). No run ever used MPC, because the scheduler pod did not have GEKKO, so MPC always ran as on/off control. The sign is fixed.
  • FLC fell back to on/off control even with scikit-fuzzy installed, because scikit-fuzzy 0.5.0 needs packaging without declaring it.

The scheduler pod now installs GEKKO, scikit-fuzzy and their dependencies, pinned by version and checksum (apps/scheduler/requirements-controllers.txt), and the health check Controller libraries warns when they are missing (Control loops; exercise E40). The libraries are deployed with ./apps/scheduler/deploy.sh while no run is active. Choosing MPC or FLC for a unit, or a new parameter set for either, is a configuration change approved by qa as before.

Held-out reservations

A reservation keeps one unit's measurements, or one zone's, with a measurement time in [from, to) out of every snapshot from then on, so a model can later be judged on data it never saw (the final validation, RS.8). Only a qa person reserves, through a command on the host:

./apps/research/reserve.sh add --by <your qa username> --unit <UNIT> [--zone <ZONE>] --from <TIME> --to <TIME> --reason "<why, 10+ characters>"
./apps/research/reserve.sh list
  • --by names the qa person making it, as their account in the platform's realm: it must exist, be enabled and hold the role qa, else "--by … does not hold the realm role qa: only qa reserves held-out data." Its username and account id are recorded with the reason.
  • TIME is ISO 8601, such as 2026-12-01 or 2026-12-01T06:00, in UTC unless it carries an offset. Without --zone every zone of the unit is held out.
  • add shows what it will hold out, says "This cannot be changed or removed afterwards." and asks for a typed yes; it takes the deploy lock. On success: "Reservation #N made: unit … [from, to) is held out of every snapshot from now on". list is read-only: "No reservations: nothing is held out of the research lake.", or a table ID, UNIT, ZONE, FROM, TO, BY, MADE, REASON (ZONE * = every zone).
  • Before the first export that would carry the data. The research database refuses a range whose measurements are already in the lake: "reserve: unit … is already exported: it is no longer held out". It also refuses a range that ends more than a year from now and a reason shorter than 10 characters. A reservation is never changed or removed.

A reservation is not an account decision and is not on the policy history: it is a row of vault.reservation in the research database (who, when, why), written as rs_owner through vault.reserve, outside the policy matrix. In ./medcea.sh: Applications → Reserve a held-out range (qa), and List the held-out reservations. The snapshot export holds a reservation out from the moment it is made; live reads by the scientists end only at the Phase 2b switchover (decision record, 17 November 2026). From Phase 2b a notebook sees which ranges are held out (unit, zone, from, to), never who made a reservation or why (Reading snapshots from a notebook).

The nightly export

  1. Asks the research database for the latest cut (lake.latest_cut(), none before the first snapshot).
  2. Takes the new cut from the operational database: its clock minus two minutes, so a reading whose transaction started before the cut has committed. It is refused when the cut is not after the latest one.

Snapshots and their digests

The lake is append-only and a snapshot is a high-water mark (owner decision S). Each export adds one row to lake.snapshot: snapshot_id, cut_at (the cut), previous_cut_at, created_at, contract_version, rows and digests. The measurements accumulate, each snapshot adding those ingested since the previous cut; the small sets (units, zones, recipes, recipe lines, runs, cycles, control loops, parameter sets, catalogue) are copied whole into every snapshot, each row tagged with its snapshot_id. close_snapshot refuses a contract version it does not know, a previous cut that is not the latest snapshot's (another export ran in between), a cut not after it, and a staged measurement outside (previous cut, cut].

  • rows: per set, its row count, plus held_out, the staged measurements a reservation kept out.
  • digests: per set, a sha256. For a small set it is the sha256 of its rows' text forms (under time zone UTC and ISO dates, sorted bytewise) joined with newlines.
  • For the measurements it is computed per hour of ingest time, so no step holds more than an hour of rows: for each hour that has rows, oldest first, the line <hour as YYYY-MM-DDTHH:00:00Z>|<rows in that hour>|<sha256 of that hour's sorted row texts joined with newlines>; the digest is the sha256 of those lines joined with newlines. A set with no rows digests the empty string.

The health check Research export (rsexport) reads the newest snapshot read-only: OK while it is at most 26 hours old (for example "newest snapshot 1 h old (snapshot 12, no reserved rows in it);2 reservation(s)"), WARN beyond, FAIL beyond 72 hours, as for backups; FAIL when rows of it fall in a held-out reservation ("… held-out data reached the lake"); WARN while there is no reservation ("no reservation yet: nothing is held out for validation …") and when something cannot be read; SKIP without the CronJob and, with no snapshot yet, during the CronJob's first 26 hours. With Research network policies it brings the health check from 66 to 68 checks.

Calibrating the sensor model from a snapshot (Phase 3)

The research plan said the chamber model's time constant, humidity and CO2 constants would be calibrated from the lake's measurements. The data does not allow it, and the owner decided on 20 November 2026 to build now and fit what is there: the whole pipeline is built (fetching a snapshot pinned by its lineage, fits that check what the data can identify, scenarios and manifests that name the snapshot), the sensor model is calibrated, and every plant constant stays an assumption that the fit refuses until step-test data exists (below). The reasons are in the data:

  • The lake's contract (version 1) exports the readings only: no state report from a CONTROL board and no command. The chamber has never had a CONTROL board, the lake holds no cycle and no control loop, and the room is not measured. A plant constant is seen only through what an actuator does to a reading against the room's conditions, so none can be fitted.
  • There is one unit and zone (UNIT01/ZONE01), about 14 hours (14 November 19:52 to 15 November 09:59 UTC), about 16,670 readings per sensor and quantity, from an SHT45 and an SCD30. There is no substrate sensor.
  • The board publishes about every 3 seconds, not every 30 as the model assumed; its silence after 15 November and the held-out range are outages, never lost messages.

Nothing here writes the lake or changes the platform: the commands read lake-api from the host and write only the files you name. There is no access policy change and no deploy.

Fitting the sensor model

python3 -m research.calib fit-sensors ~/calib/s1/dataset.jsonl --map sht45=sht45_1,scd30=scd30_1 \
    --name unit01-zone01-s1 --out ~/calib/unit01-zone01-s1.yaml --report ~/calib/report.txt

python3 -m research.calib verify ~/calib/unit01-zone01-s1.yaml

--map names which lake sensor each model sensor is. The fit takes the readings by board cycle (one timestamp per cycle, shared by every quantity of both sensors, stamped by the board) and splits them at outages (a gap of 60 seconds or more); a gap up to three cycles is normal, a longer one under 60 seconds a skipped cycle. The gaps are the board's own, read from its timestamps: the time the platform received a reading is used only for the latency, because the platform sometimes receives a run of cycles late and then all at once, which is a delivery stall, not a skipped cycle or an outage. Each value is fitted with a 95 % interval from a deterministic resampling, rounded to six significant digits, and applied only when the data supports it:

  • Publish interval: the mean of the normal gaps between the board's timestamps. The start offset is 0 for both sensors, which publish in the same cycle.
  • Latency: from the time the platform stored a reading minus the time the board measured it. It includes the board clock's offset from the host's, so it is recorded as fitted but confounded.
  • Noise: from how the second differences of a series grow with the lag, which separates the sensor's noise and the 0.01 rounding from the chamber's own slow variation. It is refused (the default stays, with the reason) when its interval includes zero, the fit is poor, or the first differences are positively correlated, which white noise on a slow process never gives: the SCD30's CO2 on snapshot 1 (correlation +0.172). The data still bounds a refused noise: white noise adds twice its variance to the first differences', so it is at most the square root of half their variance. When the model's default noise at the level the sensor read is above that bound, the data contradicts the default, and the bound is applied instead (source upper_bound, with no relative part), recorded under "not modelled" as noise that may be smaller still: the SCD30's CO2 on snapshot 1, 0.50 ppm against the datasheet's 25.6 ppm.
  • Lost messages: only a quantity missing from a cycle in which the same sensor published another; a value whose interval is wider than the value itself is weak and not applied.
  • Offsets: relative to the SHT45, the reference: it has the better datasheet accuracy, the SCD30 warms itself, and the loops use the SHT45 with the SCD30 as fallback. The SCD30 reads +2.04 °C and −7.03 %RH against it. Self-heating (the warmer sensor seeing the same water at a higher temperature) explains only about a third of how the humidity offset changes with humidity, so the model is a choice recorded in the file, humidity_model: constant_offset by default (two constant offsets), or --humidity-model self_heating (2.04 °C of self-heating plus a remaining humidity offset). Both models' predictions are recorded.
  • Absolute accuracy: no data says how far the SHT45 itself is from the truth. The file sets a per-run constant error from the datasheet (half the stated accuracy: 0.05 °C and 0.5 %RH for the SHT45, 25.6 ppm CO2 for the SCD30 at the level it read), drawn once per run from its own seeded stream.
  • Validity: the humidity range the SHT45 saw (51.35 to 55.33 %RH).
  • Not modelled, recorded for the reader: publish jitter, the delivery stalls, the skipped cycles, the chamber's fast variation, the sensors' resolution not being a clean 0.01, repeated values beyond what noise explains, and that the SCD30's absolute error is correlated with the SHT45's through the fitted offset.

The calibration file is canonical YAML: the source (dataset digest, snapshot, lineage), the code (commit, whether research/ was clean, the Python version and the blob hash of every file of the fit and the model), each value with its source (fitted, fitted_confounded, upper_bound, chosen, datasheet, not_identifiable), interval, and applied or the reason it was not, and one entry per plant constant with its current value and why it is refused. fit-sensors and fit-plant refuse while the code they run and record (research/calib/*.py, research/twin_plant/*.py) differs from the commit, unless --allow-dirty, which the file records; a calibration or extract written under research/calib/calibrations/ or research/calib/extracts/ is data and does not count, so the next fit runs without committing it first. verify checks a file offline: its form, every rule above, and whether its code still equals the checkout.

fit-plant lists every plant constant with what the dataset lacks and exits 3, writing nothing, while none is identifiable. On snapshot 1 every constant but the volume (a hardware fact) gets NO_ACTUATOR_DATA (the lake has no command or state report), NO_EXCITATION (no actuator step) and NO_AMBIENT (the room is not measured); the substrate's add NOT_OBSERVED, the fungi's respiration and water CONDITIONAL_REFUSED (they need a closed-chamber relaxation with the room and substrate measured), and the biological figures LITERATURE. The step-fit itself is built and tested on simulated step tests; a calibration file with a fitted plant value is refused in Phase 3 ("plant fits are applied from a later phase"), and one whose assumed value differs from the model's is refused too (change an assumption in the scenario, where it is visible).

How the agent will work (Phase 4, design)

Phase 4 adds a supervised agent: a scientist starts it from a notebook, it plans simulations and runs them within a CPU budget, reads lake snapshots, and ends with a proposal. Every step is recorded in a tamper-evident trace that can be replayed, and nothing it produces reaches production without qa. The figure is the design decided on 21 November 2026, and today all of it runs except the Phase 6 promotion path at the bottom. The box styles show when each part was built: solid boxes ran before Phase 4, dashed grey boxes came in 4a (with a scripted agent, no language model), and dashed amber boxes came in 4b (the local model, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507) or are still to come (Phase 6). The numbers follow the order of the work.

The work, in order.

  1. Start. In a notebook, the scientist runs medcea-agent start with a profile (for example "compare controllers") and a CPU budget.
  2. Sign in and get a session. The orchestrator relays a device sign-in to research-session, which checks the person and their scientist role with Keycloak and mints a session token: up to 8 hours, a fixed tool list and budget, at most two sessions per person.
  3. The agent starts. The orchestrator starts one agent Job. Only that pod can claim the token, which is bound to its address and revoked when the Job ends.
  4. Think. Each turn goes to the model through the gateway: the local Qwen3-4B (or another endpoint chosen for the session), or a scripted agent. Tool output is wrapped as untrusted data, so text inside a result is never taken as an instruction.
  5. Plan a campaign. The agent asks mcp-sim for an estimate. mcp-sim recomputes the CPU cost itself and applies the gate: below 2 CPU-hours it runs at once; above, the scientist approves it in the CLI; above 24 it is refused.
  6. Simulate. A simulation Job runs the chamber twin with production's own controllers, under a hard CPU-hour cap and deadline, and reports metrics and a result digest.
  7. Look at real data. Through mcp-lake the agent reads person-free lake snapshots with fixed queries, never the live platform.
  8. Everything is recorded. Every turn, tool call and result, gate decision and proposal is appended by research-session to the agent_trace hash chain. The nightly backup anchors its head, and a replay of the session must give the same results byte for byte.
  9. A proposal, never a change. The agent ends with a proposal (for example a tuned PID parameter set) that stays in the trace. From Phase 6 an engineer can import it as a draft, and only qa's approval lets a unit run it.

What keeps it safe.

  • Proposals only. The agent has no route to the scheduler, the boards, MQTT or the recipes. Its output is a record to review.
  • Budgets and a human gate. Turns, tokens, CPU-hours and wall time are capped per session (16 CPU-hours by default). Large campaigns wait for the scientist's approval, and the caps are enforced while simulations run, not only estimated.
  • Isolation. The agent machinery runs in its own namespace, which the notebook hub cannot create pods in. The signing key and the trace writer live in a third. The agent has no service account, no secret and no internet.
  • Tamper-evident and replayable. The trace is append-only and hash-chained, written only by research-session, with the writer chosen by network port, never by what the agent claims. Strict replay checks every recorded step.
  • Production comes first. Everything runs at the lowest priority, inside quotas, with the model on 6 cores of one processor. Governance (AI inventory, a risk assessment per profile, the oversight procedure) is recorded before the first session.

Names are the design's (codeplan/rs4-design.md, with its revisions after review). Before 4a was built, step 0 measured the local model on this host (Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B on llama.cpp, processors with AVX only; 21 Nov 2026).

The local model (Phase 4b, stages 1 and 2)

The agent can think with a language model that runs on this host's CPU, inside the platform: Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (Q4_K_M, Apache-2.0), served by llama.cpp (server-b12182, by digest). The owner chose the 4B model and a context of 4096 tokens after the measurements of step 0, so a local session is a short one. Stage 1 built the model's server, its switch and its health check; stage 2 added the gateway that connects an agent to it and the agent's local backend (below). The access policy version that admits both pods is version 12 (the Phase 4b skeleton), applied; both pods run.

What runs. ./apps/research-model/deploy.sh puts Deployment and Service research-llm in the research gateway namespace (RESEARCH_GW_NAMESPACE): one llama-server, one slot, greedy sampling, no API key and no internet. It asks for 6 CPUs (no CPU limit, low priority) and 6 GiB of memory; about 3.7 GB is in use at a 4096-token context. An init container checks the model file's sha256 against the pin and that the CPU has AVX at every start, so a wrong file never starts a server. The file (about 2.5 GB) lives on the host in RESEARCH_MODEL_DIR (default /var/lib/<project>/research-models, mode 700, yours) and reaches the pod as a read-only volume; the deploy downloads it through ./apps/research-model/bench.sh fetch when it is missing (resumable, checked against the pin).

The gateway and the local backend (stage 2). A session never talks to research-llm itself: it asks model-gateway (Deployment and Service in the same namespace, port 8120, installed by the same ./apps/research-model/deploy.sh after the model; its code is the ConfigMap model-gateway-code, and uninstalling removes it first). For each turn the agent sends the gateway the session token and the conversation so far. The gateway checks the token with the session service (a token only works from the pod it was given to), refuses a session whose prompt or output tokens are spent, translates the conversation into the model server's chat request, and answers with the model's text and at most one tool call. The request is always the same fixed greedy setting (temperature 0, top-k 1, seed 1, thinking off, prompt cache on); the answer length is whatever the session has left, at most 400 tokens for the local model (one tool call is about 60 to 120; at the 1.2 to 1.4 tokens a second this host generates at depth, 400 tokens is about five minutes, within the gateway's 540-second wait) and at most 1,024 for another endpoint. An answer cut off at that limit without a tool call comes back as a refused call ("the answer was cut off …"), so the model can try again; until 24 November 2026 the limit for the local model was 1,024, and a runaway answer ran into the wait and ended the session with model_error. Same request, same answer holds only for the same cache state. The model server keeps the previous conversation in its prompt cache; a request whose beginning it already holds is computed from the cached state in other batches than a fresh one, the arithmetic differs in the last digits, and greedy decoding can then pick another token. On 24 November 2026 the same turn-1 request (same sha256) gave a 120-token tool call from a cold cache and, the next session, a 700-token runaway from a warm one. Replay does not depend on this (it uses the recorded answers); re-execution does, so it is run right after the session it repeats. There is one slot: while one turn runs, another session is told busy (429) and the agent waits and asks again, the wait counting against its wall-clock cap. The gateway asks for one call per answer (parallel_tool_calls false); if an answer still holds several, the first is taken and the number of the others is recorded beside the answer (dropped_calls). A call the model wrote badly (a tool that does not exist, arguments that are not an object, a call written as plain text) comes back as text flagged as an error; the agent treats it as a refused call and the model sees the refusal on its next turn. An answer without a tool call ends the session, except while none of the profile's finish_after tools has succeeded (for the local profile, propose.parameter_set): then the agent answers once (nudges) that the task is not finished and asks for one tool call, and a second such answer stops the session with no_tool_call. If the model's context is full the session stops with context_full. The loop estimates each request at 4 bytes a token; through the gateway it scales that estimate by the model's own count of the last request and does not start a turn that would leave less than the profile's caps.reserve_tokens for the answer (300 for the local profile): on 25 November 2026 the bytes estimate let a proposal run into the end of the context, where the model server cut it off.

What the model is told about a campaign. The tools sim.estimate and sim.run describe the campaign spec in full in their schema: scenarios (scenario files), controllers (each a code of IF_THEN_ELSE, PID, FLC, MPC or Q_LEARNING, with optional params and params_grid), seeds, and optionally clock_offsets_s, jitter_s and description; a refused spec answers with the same shape. Until 23 November 2026 the schema said only "an object", and the local model, which sees nothing else, guessed a flat spec that was refused (exercise E49). A session started before that change replays only against the tool listing it ran with.

What is recorded, and by whom. For a local session the agent does not write the model's two records; the gateway does, under its own name (model-gateway): turn_request (the request's digest, the model pin and the fixed settings) before the call and turn_response (the response, its digest, the raw answer's digest and the model server's own token counts) after it. If the trace refuses a record, the turn is refused and nothing is returned that the trace does not hold. A failed model call is recorded as an error response. Everything else (tool calls, results, checkpoints, the stop) is the agent's, as in a scripted session. The profile compare_controllers_local asks for the same tools and the same CPU-hour cap as the scripted one, with 8 turns, 2 hours of wall-clock and 4,000 tokens of context (a local profile may not ask for more than 4,096); the task is the first message of the conversation. The orchestrator refuses to start a local profile (409) while no gateway is configured, that is while RESEARCH_LOCAL_MODEL is not true when ./apps/research-agents/deploy.sh runs.

Replay. A local session is replayed from the gateway's recorded responses instead of the agent's, and each rebuilt request must have the digest the gateway recorded. That proves the agent loop, the tool results and the caps behaved as recorded. It does not prove that the model would answer the same again: running the model a second time is a separate measurement, never the evidence.

The switch. RESEARCH_LOCAL_MODEL (false by default; an older config.env without the line reads false). Switch it with ./medcea.sh → Applications → Turn the local model on or off: enable sets it true, runs ./apps/research-model/deploy.sh and ./health-check.sh; rollback sets it false and runs ./apps/research-model/uninstall.sh, which removes the server and keeps the model file. Endpoints (research Phase 4d, 23 November 2026). A profile's backend is scripted or the name of a model endpoint; local is the model on this host. local is listed in apps/research-model/endpoints.json; every other endpoint is this install's configuration, not code (25 November 2026): config.env holds, for an endpoint name (lower-case letters, digits and -; in the variable names upper-cased with _), RESEARCH_ENDPOINT_<NAME>_URL (http(s)://host:port, no /v1), _MODEL, _CONTEXT, optional _MAX_OUTPUT, _DETERMINISTIC (true when the server honours a seed, as vLLM does), optional _THINKING (false switches Qwen3's thinking off), _TOOLS (native, the server's own tool calls, or text, for a server started without them: the tools go into the system message in the Hermes form Qwen's chat template uses and the call is read back from <tool_call> text; the model's earlier calls are written back into the conversation name first, {"name": .., "arguments": {..}}, as the instructions show, and a call the gateway cannot read is refused with a quote of what the model wrote, so that it can correct it. Until 26 November 2026 the history listed the arguments first: Qwen3-14B copied that order, put the name inside the arguments, and repeated the unreadable call until its turns ran out (E58)) and optional _KEY, the API key. An endpoint is enabled by naming it in RESEARCH_ENDPOINTS. ./apps/research-model/deploy.sh passes these variables to its helper (endpoints_config.py) through the environment, never on a command line, checks them, writes the endpoint list into the gateway's ConfigMap and the keys into Secret model-gateway-keys in the gateway's namespace, mounted into the gateway only (never into a session; health Research grants and Secrets allows that Secret to the gateway and nothing else); a key is never printed or logged. The gateway talks to such a server with temperature 0, a seed only where the endpoint is deterministic, one call per answer, and a context overflow (vLLM's "maximum context length") recorded as context_full. Every turn's record names the endpoint, its model and these settings. A private server also needs its address admitted by the access policy (a skeleton version approved by qa) before the gateway can reach it: the research zones refuse every private address (100.64.0.0/10, Tailscale's, included), and the firewall rule is built from the policy, so the address is written there and nowhere in the code. Hosted APIs (Anthropic, OpenAI) come with the hosted-model conduit and its decision record.

Choosing the model (the model picker, 25 November 2026). A scientist chooses which model a session uses. c.endpoints() in a notebook (or medcea-agent endpoints) lists the enabled endpoints in the order RESEARCH_ENDPOINTS names them, each with its name, model, context, whether it is deterministic and where it runs ("this host (CPU)" or "another server"), never an address or a key. c.start(profile, endpoint="gpu") (or medcea-agent start PROFILE --endpoint gpu) runs the profile on that model instead of its own; leaving it out keeps the profile's. The choice is the session's backend: the orchestrator refuses an endpoint that is not enabled, and a choice for a scripted profile; the gateway serves the session from that endpoint only; the trace's every turn names it. The same instructions, tools, budget and gate apply whichever model answers; only the context follows the model: on its own endpoint the profile's caps.context_tokens, on another that endpoint's context less 96 tokens (the chat template's own). Replay takes the endpoint and its context from the gateway's record, and re-execution runs on the model the session ran on. The list reaches the orchestrator at ./apps/research-agents/deploy.sh (it is part of the existing GET /api/sessions answer: no route of its own, so no change to the access policy). The research console, when it is built, shows the same list.

A proposal is checked against its campaign (26 November 2026). propose.parameter_set takes, besides the controller, its parameters and the campaigns that back it, a criterion: what the setting does better, lower being better for all of them, either energy_wh (the run's total energy) or a loop's metric, <loop>.rmsd_mid, <loop>.out_of_band or <loop>.boundary_hugging (for example air_temperature.rmsd_mid). mcp-sim, not the model, then compares the proposed setting with every other setting of those campaigns on the runs both have (same scenario, seed, clock offset and jitter; valid runs only), by the mean of the criterion. A scenario is what the campaign ran, not its name: the scenario as resolved from its file or the spec, with the campaign's code, so runs of two campaigns meet only on the same scenario under the same code, and a campaign whose scenarios cannot be resolved is compared within itself. A parameter a run's spec did not name counts at its default (a result row holds only the values its spec named), so PID with no parameters and PID with cycle_time 10 are one setting. It refuses the proposal when the setting was not run, has no valid run, shares no run with another setting, only ties every other setting, or another setting does better; the refusal names that setting and both numbers, so the agent can propose it instead. It also refuses a campaign whose runs it does not all hold (it keeps 50 per campaign). The comparison is written into the proposal document (check: the criterion, the proposed setting's mean and, per setting compared, the runs shared, both means and whether the proposed setting is better or tied), so the supervisor, and from Phase 6 the engineer and qa, read the server's numbers; the rationale stays the model's own words. It came from E57: a proposal whose rationale called PID's 84.07 Wh lower energy than IF_THEN_ELSE's 28.33 Wh. The shipped profiles name their criterion: the scripted compare_controllers proposes PID with cycle_time 30 on air_temperature.rmsd_mid (it beats 10 and IF_THEN_ELSE on seed 1), the local one asks for the PID setting that did better on that criterion.

A profile for a larger model (26 November 2026). compare_controllers_gpu runs on the endpoint named gpu with 12,000 tokens of context, 14 turns, a 300-second turn and an 8,000-token output budget: all five controllers (IF_THEN_ELSE, PID at cycle_time 10 and 30, FLC, MPC, Q_LEARNING) on the smoke scenario with seeds 1, 2 and 3 and clock offsets 0 and 5 s in one campaign (36 runs, about 0.1 CPU-hours: below the gate), then one proposal on air_temperature.rmsd_mid whose rationale says how the setting compares on energy_wh. Its skill tells the model to propose the setting a refusal names. The orchestrator refuses it (409) where no endpoint called gpu is enabled, and where that endpoint holds fewer tokens than the profile's context plus the chat template's 96; any profile run on its own endpoint is checked the same way, whether that endpoint is left out or named (as re-execution names it); the local one fits the local model exactly (4,000 of 4,096). With the model picker it can run on another endpoint, whose context then caps it.

The research console (Phase 4e)

Built 27 November 2026, not deployed (branch research/phase-4e, commits A1a, A1b, A3, A2 and B; owner decisions OD-4e-1 to OD-4e-11 of 27 November 2026, in the decision record). The skeleton version that gives the console its address is written but not yet proposed; until qa approves it and it is applied, the portal's Agent campaigns card stays greyed out and ./apps/research-console/deploy.sh installs nothing. Exercise E59 follows the deploy.

The research console is a web page for supervising agent sessions, at https://agents.<domain> (RESEARCH_CONSOLE_DOMAIN), opened from the portal's Agent campaigns card in the Research area. It does what medcea_agent does from a notebook, and shows what a session did as a picture rather than as lines of text. The command medcea_agent keeps working: the console uses the same routes of the orchestrator, and every answer only gained fields.

Who opens it, and what they see. The console admits the roles that hold the group research_campaigns: bio_scientist and control_scientist (Read, Create, Update: they start and steer sessions) and engineer (Read only). Every other role gets the sign-in proxy's "No access to Research console" page, which names the roles that may open it. Each session is visible only to the person who started it (owner decision OD-4e-3): a scientist sees their own sessions and nobody else's, and an engineer, who starts none, sees an empty list with the sentence "Sessions are shown to the person who started them". Another person's session id answers 404, as it does in a notebook.

The views

  • Sessions (the start page): your running sessions and your older ones, newest first (Older sessions pages back), each with its state (running, ended with its reason, revoked, expired), profile, model, start time and what it has spent against its budget (turns, prompt and output tokens, CPU-hours) as bars. Start a session: choose a Profile and a Model (the model picker: the endpoints this install offers, as c.endpoints() lists them) and press Start. The page refuses at once a profile whose context does not fit the chosen model (its context less 96 tokens), with the orchestrator's own words. A role without Create sees no form.
  • Session: the header with the budget, the state and the buttons Stop (while it runs), Replay (once it has stopped), Re-execute and Download OTel. Below it:
    • Agents: the agents of the session as a graph: the lead (a0), its specialists (a0.1, a0.2 …, see Agent teams) and the servers they used (the model gateway, mcp-sim, mcp-lake), with the number of calls on each edge. Click an agent to show only its lanes and events.
    • Timeline: one lane per agent, per server and one for you, the supervisor. A bar is a span: a model turn (asked to answered), a tool call (called to its result), a campaign (started to finished), a gate's wait (pending to decided). Marks show proposals, checkpoints, the stop, a refused trace write and the revocation. The agent's sim.status checks while a campaign runs fold into one "waiting" bar with their count, as watch folds them. A span still open is drawn to now. Zoom in, Zoom out and Whole session move the view.
    • Inspector: click any mark or bar. It shows the event: its id, time, actor, agent, payload and digests; for a tool result the at most 1,000 characters the model was shown beside the full record (at most 6,000) and the tool server's own record of the same call; for a turn the request and response digests and the tokens used; the event's prev_hash and hash in the chain.
    • Gates, campaigns and proposals: each gate with its estimate and cap, and while it waits Approve (with Cap (CPU-h)) or Deny, each with a note. The page refuses a cap that is not above 0, above 24 CPU-hours, above what the session has left, or above 1.25 × the estimate plus the calibration, before it asks the server (which checks the same). Each proposal with its document, its check (the proposed setting against every other setting on the shared runs: means, runs, better or tied) and the model's rationale, and while undecided Accept or Discard, with a note. Only the session's own supervisor sees the buttons; a role without Update sees none ("Your role does not decide gates").
  • Campaign (open a campaign): its manifest, its by_setting table as a dot plot per Criterion (energy_wh and each loop's rmsd_mid, out_of_band, boundary_hugging), the proposed setting marked when a proposal cites the campaign, and the runs in a table (paged, sortable). A campaign not finished yet says so in its own state: waiting for the supervisor, approved and not started yet, running, or denied. Its result is shown only when the file on the runs volume has the digest the trace recorded; otherwise the page says the results do not match the trace (409), or that they are gone (410) while the trace still names them.
  • Run (open a run): its result row and its chamber series: the simulated air temperature, humidity, CO2 and substrate temperature over time with each loop's setpoint band shaded, the loop's value, its commands and the relays as step strips, the light, the energy so far, the line where the metrics start, and the disturbances.
  • Replay: runs the replay of a stopped session (as c.replay(sid)) and shows The record beside the verdict, scrolled to the first mismatch (its event, field, agent, what was recorded and what the replay produced). "The replay matches the record" when it does.
  • Re-execute: starts the same profile on the session's own model as a new session (the new session records which session it re-executes), then compares the two turn by turn and marks the first turn that differs. Both sessions must be yours. Only a session that ran on a model can be re-executed; a scripted one is refused (409 "the session is not a model session: nothing to re-execute"), since its answers come from its profile.

The chamber series, and switching it off

A simulation run records the chamber's state every 60 seconds of simulated time (at most 5,000 points a run: a longer scenario gets a wider step), into series/<n>.json.gz beside the campaign's results.jsonl on the research-runs volume. The series is outside every digest: a campaign's spec and result digests are the same with or without it, so pinned results never move. It is kept until ./apps/research-agents/uninstall.sh --purge-data removes the volume (nothing prunes it). The step is RS_SIM_SERIES_EVERY (seconds, default 60, 0 = off), read by ./apps/research-agents/deploy.sh; a campaign run without a series shows "no series recorded for this campaign" when you open a run. A whole campaign is about 5 MB of series for 45 runs of 48 hours.

Live updates, and how soon a lost role takes effect

An open session follows its trace live: the page asks the orchestrator for events after the last one it has and the orchestrator holds the answer up to 20 seconds until one arrives; after two failed waits it asks every 3 seconds instead, and after the session stops every 15 seconds for 2 more minutes (to catch the campaign's end and the replay verdict). A hidden tab stops asking. Each wait is also bounded by your sign-in token's expiry, and the console's sign-in proxy renews the token every 4 minutes: a person whose role is removed, or whose account is deactivated, stops seeing new events within about 5 minutes.

Downloading a session as OpenTelemetry

Download OTel saves the session as an OpenTelemetry trace (OTLP/JSON, session-<sid>-otel.json) for Jaeger (its "JSON file" upload) or Grafana with a Tempo data source. It is derived from the record, never read back: the hash-chained trace stays the evidence, and the export names the chain head it was made from. One trace per session, always with the same ids; a span for the session, one per agent, per model turn (chat), per tool call (execute_tool) and per campaign, after the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions (v1.37.0). By default it holds digests and sizes, never the prompt, the model's text or a tool result; with content (a tick beside the button) adds them as the trace holds them (the file is then session-<sid>-otel-content.json), and the orchestrator's log records that you asked (the session and your username). It never holds a token, the claim or your account's id; the supervisor appears as role and username. From a notebook: c.otel(sid), or medcea-agent otel SID [--content] > file.json.

How it is built

  • Its own pod, and no token. Deployment research-console in the agent namespace serves only the page (HTML, script, styles and two libraries) from ConfigMap research-console-html. It holds no Kubernetes token and no Secret, reaches nothing, and checks every request against the route table like every other service. Its sign-in proxy research-console-oauth2-proxy (platform namespace) sends / to it and /api/ straight to the orchestrator, with your token; your browser holds only the proxy's cookie.
  • One audience. Keycloak client research-console gives tokens only for medcea-research, the audience the orchestrator and the session service already verify; the console's own server verifies it too. The console therefore has its own Sign out (top right) and no user menu.
  • Untrusted text. Whatever a model wrote or a tool answered may contain HTML. The page never inserts it as HTML, and its Content Security Policy runs only its own scripts: an injected <script> is shown as text and never runs.
  • Cross-site requests. The orchestrator refuses a POST whose Origin is another site's (403 "wrong origin"), as well as one that is not JSON (415). A request without Origin (the notebook's) passes.
  • The libraries. uPlot 1.6.32 (the series and the plots) and Cytoscape.js 3.35.0 (the agents graph), both MIT, copied unchanged from their npm packages with each file's sha256 in a VERSION file; the health check compares them. Nothing is fetched from the internet when the page opens.

The health check Research console (rsconsole; 78 checks with it) proves the page server serves what its ConfigMap holds, refuses / without a token, serves the vendored files of the VERSION records, and has no Secret or token; it SKIPs until the applied policy has the console's address. Agent teams (Phase 4e)

Built 27 November 2026 with the console (commits A1a and A1b; owner decisions OD-4e-4 and OD-4e-5), not deployed. A session may have more than one agent: a lead that hands parts of its task to specialists.

  • Agent ids. The lead is a0; its specialists are a0.1, a0.2 and so on (at most three levels below the lead). Every event an agent writes names its agent, and a specialist's also its parent; the trace refuses an id of another form. A session recorded before 27 November 2026 has no ids and reads as the lead alone, and still replays. The id is not part of what the model is asked, so request digests do not change.
  • Delegation. A team profile lists its specialists under team:: for each a description, a skill, its tools (a subset of the lead's, never team.delegate: a specialist does not delegate) and its caps. The lead's model gets the tool team.delegate {specialist, task}. It is served inside the agent's own Job, never by a tool server: the specialist runs there as a sub-agent, to its end, before the lead goes on (one at a time; parallel specialists come with the control research track, Phase 5c). Its start and end are the events agent_spawned and agent_returned; its last answer (at most 1,000 characters, with the newest campaign it ran) comes back to the lead as the tool's result. An unknown specialist, a bad task or no budget left is a refused call, never an error.
  • One budget. Every agent's turns, tokens and CPU-hours count against the session's caps: a specialist's own caps are never larger than the lead's, nor than what the lead has left; the lead's wall clock ends it too. A campaign's CPU counts once, whoever ran it (and whether mcp-sim or the orchestrator recorded its end).
  • Replay. A team session replays: each specialist runs again from its recorded answers and results, and a mismatch inside a specialist names it.
  • The team profile. compare_controllers_team (scripted): the lead looks at the lake, hands the campaign to the specialist campaign (estimate, run, read until done) and the proposal to tuning (PID cycle_time 30 on air_temperature.rmsd_mid, with the campaign as evidence). In the console it is three agents on the graph. medcea-agent watch (c.watch) indents a specialist's lines by depth and ends with the team in one line.

Other data added for the console (commit A1a, no policy change): a gate or proposal decision must name one that exists and is still undecided (404 "no such gate" or "no such proposal", 409 "this gate is already decided" or "this proposal is already decided"); a proposal's id is p<event id> (an older hexadecimal id still works); decisions and gates are found past the first 1,000 events; your sessions are listed from the database, so the list survives a restart of the orchestrator; the orchestrator records sim_finished when a campaign's Job ends.
