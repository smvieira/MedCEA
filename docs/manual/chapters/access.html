Getting access and roles

Every person signs in to MedCEA with one personal account, and every account holds exactly one platform role. You ask for an account on the sign-in page. An administrator reviews the request, and a different person holding qa confirms it before the account can be used. Your role decides which web apps you can open and what you may do in them. This chapter covers how to request access, what each of the eleven roles is for, how to sign in and out, how to manage your profile and password, what happens when your role changes or your account is deactivated, and what to do when a page says you have no access.

Requesting access

The platform has one sign-in page, served by Keycloak in the MedCEA look. It has two tabs: Sign in and Request access. Below the sign-in form there is also a link, "No account yet? Request access". Opening https://access.<domain>/register brings you to the same tab.

What the form asks

  1. Open the sign-in page and choose the Request access tab.
  2. Fill in every field and choose the role you need.
  3. Choose Submit request. The page answers "Request received. You will get an e-mail once an administrator has reviewed it."
  4. Wait for the e-mail. Until then, signing in shows "This account is not active".

The form refuses a request when another request for the same e-mail address is already waiting, or when the username or e-mail address already has an account. One network address can send at most 10 valid requests per hour.

What happens to your request

Submitting creates your account at once, but disabled. It stays disabled until two different people have acted on it:

  1. An administrator opens the request in the User Manager under Requests, and either approves it with a role (the one you asked for or a different one) or rejects it with a note explaining why. The administrator also writes the e-mail you will receive, starting from a prepared text, or unticks Send e-mail to (followed by your address) so that none is sent.
  2. An approval does not enable the account. It becomes an account decision that waits under Decisions until a person holding qa confirms or refuses it. That person must not be the administrator who approved it, and must not be you.
  3. When qa confirms, the account is enabled with the granted role and the e-mail the administrator wrote is sent. If the granted role differs from the one you asked for, the prepared text the administrator starts from says the role was reassigned.

A rejection, or a qa refusal of the approval, deletes the disabled account. You may apply again.

E-mails depend on the platform's mail account, which an administrator sets up in the User Manager under E-mail settings. Without it, the decision is still recorded but no e-mail goes out, so ask an administrator directly.

Your request is stored with every field except the password, which only Keycloak keeps. Each step (submitted, approved, reassigned, rejected) is added to the request's history, with who decided and when. The approval and the qa confirmation are kept as one account decision that names the maker, the checker, both times and any notes, and the e-mail text the administrator wrote is stored with the request.

The roles come from the access policy (apps/iam/policy/defaults.yaml, section roles). The role key is the name the platform uses in the User Manager and in records. One platform role per account

An account holds exactly one platform role. To do work that needs another role, ask an administrator to change your role; you do not get a second one. When a role changes, the new role is added before the old one is removed, so a failure in between leaves the account with two roles for a moment, never with none.

Two rules protect the separation between administration and approval:

  • No person may hold an admin account and a qa account at the same time. The User Manager checks this against all of a person's accounts, enabled or not, when a role is changed and again when the change is confirmed.
  • The User Manager refuses to take away the last enabled admin or the last enabled qa.

Operators and their units

An operator works only in the units assigned to them: they see the telemetry, runs and run logs of those units and start or stop runs only there. With no unit assigned they see none of this. The administrator assigns units on the platform host with apps/iam/units.sh, not in the User Manager. Each assignment waits until a qa person other than the administrator confirms it in the User Manager under Decisions; a removal takes effect at once. A production_manager is not limited to assigned units. The Administration chapter describes how assignments are made.

Opening a web app is only the first gate. What you may do inside it (create a recipe, start a run, approve a change, export data) is decided again for each action by the service and by the database, from the same policy. For example, every role can open the Configuration UI, but only some roles can save a draft there and only qa can approve one.

The service portal shows every card to everyone. A card for an app your role cannot open leads to the "No access" page described in Why can't I open an app.

Signing in and out

  1. Open any platform address, for example the service portal. If you are not signed in, you are sent to the sign-in page.
  2. On the Sign in tab, enter your Username or e-mail and your Password. Show displays the password as you type it.
  3. Tick Remember me if you want Keycloak to keep your sign-in on this browser after you close it.
  4. Choose Sign in. You return to the page you asked for.

One sign-in covers every web app on the platform: once signed in, opening another app does not ask for your password again. pgAdmin, too, goes straight to the platform sign-in; it shows its MedCEA sign-in button only after you used pgAdmin's own Logout, and choosing it uses the same sign-in.

Messages you may see on the sign-in page:

  • "Invalid username or password." Check both and try again.
  • "This account is not active." Your request has not been confirmed yet, or your account was deactivated. Contact an administrator.

To sign out, open the user menu and choose Sign out. This ends your platform sign-in session, not only the page you are on:

  1. The app you are in signs you out of itself at once.
  2. The sign-in page asks "Do you want to log out?". Confirm it. This ends your platform sign-in.
  3. When the AI Workbench is installed, you then pass through the workbench, which ends its own session. This happens even if you never opened the workbench.
  4. You land on the service portal's sign-in page.

Without the AI Workbench, Sign out works as before. After the "Do you want to log out?" page it returns you to the app you signed out from, which asks you to sign in again.

File → Log Out in the AI Workbench, Logout in the Node-RED editor and sign out on the onboarding wizard's page also end your platform sign-in in the same way, not only that app.

The other apps keep a short session of their own. When it ends, each app checks with the platform sign-in again, without asking for your password while that sign-in lasts, and follows whoever is signed in now:

Switching accounts in one browser

After Sign out, or when someone else signs in on the same browser, every app follows the new person within the times in the table above. Until then, an app you had open may still show the previous person. These are known gaps:

  • The AI Workbench may show the previous person for up to 5 minutes (an open JupyterLab tab for up to 10 minutes). The new person is never shown the previous person's workbench server: the workbench refuses it.
  • Grafana may show the previous person for up to 5 minutes after its tab was last used, and for up to an hour after they opened it while a tab keeps it in use (for example a dashboard that refreshes itself). Close Grafana's tabs before you hand over the computer.
  • The Node-RED editor may show the previous person for up to an hour after they opened it, and acts as that person, when the new person may also open Node-RED. Editor sessions started before 1 November 2026 were set to last a week; the deploy of that day ended them.
  • pgAdmin may show the previous person until it has gone 60 minutes without use, and for at most a day. Use pgAdmin's own Logout before you hand over the computer.
  • The app you chose Sign out in is signed out at once. Another app you had open in another tab can keep working in that tab for up to 4 minutes.
  • Open live connections stay connected until you close their tab. These are JupyterLab kernels, MQTT Explorer's broker connection and Grafana's live updates.

On a shared computer, do not tick Remember me. Sign out, then close the browser, or use a private window for each person.

The user menu and your profile

Signed-in pages (the service portal, the Configuration UI, the Scheduler, the Monitor, Database Management, MQTT Explorer and the User Manager) show your name with your initials at the top right. Choosing it opens the user menu:

  • Profile opens your profile page.
  • Sign out signs you out.

If the profile service cannot be reached, the menu shows "Account" instead of your name and still offers Sign out. In that case Sign out ends your sign-in for that app only, so close the browser if you need to be sure you are signed out everywhere.

If Sign out stops on an error page of the AI Workbench after you confirmed "Do you want to log out?", your platform sign-in has already ended; only the workbench did not answer. Its own session ends by itself within 5 minutes, and the other apps follow within the times in the table above. Close the browser if you need to be sure, and tell an administrator that the workbench did not answer.

Profile

The profile page, "Your MedCEA account", has two parts:

  • Personal details: you can change your first name, last name and organisation and choose Save changes. Your username and e-mail are shown but cannot be changed here; ask an administrator.
  • Password: Change password takes you to the platform's password page. You may be asked to sign in again first. When the change is done you return to your profile, which says "Your password was changed." The password goes only to Keycloak, never through the platform's own services.

The Back to link at the top left (for example "Back to portal") returns you to the page you opened the menu from, or to that app's home page.

Forgotten password

The sign-in page shows a Forgot password? link only when the platform's mail account is configured and confirmed. Then:

  1. Choose Forgot password? on the sign-in page.
  2. Enter your username or e-mail address on the "Reset your password" page.
  3. Follow the link in the e-mail you receive and set a new password.

When the link is missing, mail is not configured: ask an administrator for help.

An administrator sets the mail account in the User Manager under E-mail settings. The change takes effect, and the Forgot password? link appears, only after a qa person confirms it under Decisions. This stops an administrator from sending password-reset mail through a mailbox they read.

When your role changes or your account is deactivated

Only an administrator changes a role, activates or deactivates an account, in the User Manager under Users. A role change and a deactivation need a reason; for an activation the reason is optional. An administrator cannot change their own account.

ChangeWhen it takes effect
Role change that gives more accessWaits under Decisions until a qa person confirms it. When it is confirmed, any MQTT Explorer credentials you hold are revoked; the Explorer then asks for a new one with your new role.
Role change that only removes accessApplied at once. Any MQTT Explorer credentials you hold are revoked: the broker refuses them within about 15 seconds.
Activation of a disabled accountAlways waits for a qa confirmation.
DeactivationApplied at once. Every platform sign-in session is ended (Grafana, pgAdmin, Superset and AI Workbench sessions as described below the table), MQTT Explorer credentials are revoked (the broker refuses them within about 15 seconds, and no new one is issued on the old sign-in), and any decision still waiting about the account is withdrawn, except unit assignments.

You do not need to sign out for a role change to reach you. The sign-in gate in front of each app checks your session with Keycloak every 4 minutes, and the proof of sign-in passed to the services is valid for 5 minutes. So, for the apps behind such a gate (all except Grafana, Superset, the AI Workbench and pgAdmin, which keep their own sessions; the AI Workbench is described below the list):

  • after a role change, the apps you can open and the actions you may take follow the new role within about 4 minutes;
  • after a deactivation, your sessions end at once, and a page you still have open stops working within about 4 minutes, at the next check.

The AI Workbench checks your role each time you sign in to it, and a workbench session lasts 5 minutes before the sign-in runs again (without a password while your platform session is open). So a role that no longer includes the workbench, or a deactivation, reaches it within 5 minutes; a notebook tab left open stops saving within about 10 minutes. Your files are kept, and come back if access is restored. A notebook that reads platform data with your own sign-in (Reading platform data from a notebook) follows the platform's 5-minute rule instead: after a role change its next requests run with the new role, and after a deactivation they are refused within five minutes.

The device onboarding wizard checks your role when you sign in and again each time it renews your sign-in during a run (at least every five minutes). If you lose engineer or maintenance during a run, it stops before the board is erased. pgAdmin checks your role only when you sign in: a pgAdmin session you already have open stays open after a role change until it ends, an hour after pgAdmin last saved it. Superset (installed since 8 November 2026) likewise checks your role only when you sign in: a session already open keeps its Superset role after a role change or a deactivation until it ends, 5 minutes after it was last used and at the latest one hour after its sign-in.

Grafana also checks your role only when you sign in. A Grafana tab you already have open keeps the Grafana role it was given, and keeps showing every unit's telemetry, after a role change or a deactivation, until that Grafana session ends (5 minutes after the tab was last used, at the latest one hour after you signed in to Grafana) or Grafana restarts (it restarts when the policy changes who gets which Grafana role). Then Grafana sends you through the sign-in again, which checks your role. This is a known gap.

Every role change, activation and deactivation is kept as an account decision, with the reason, who made it, who confirmed it when confirmation is needed, and the times. Each one that takes effect is also added to the account's status history, with the old and new role for a role change. These histories can only be added to, never edited.

Changing the access policy

Who holds which level on what is the access policy. Since slice 4.4 (November 2026) a change to it is made by one person and approved by another, in the Access Policy:

StepWhoWhere
Draft and edit a change of the matrix (who holds which level), submit it, withdraw itadminAccess Policy → Changes and Matrix
Propose a change of the platform skeleton (roles, groups, routes, hosts, network rules) from a git commitThe host operator./apps/iam/apply.sh --propose-skeleton … --from-git <commit> (see apply.sh)
Approve or return a submitted versionA qa person who is none of its authors and whose role it does not widen; for a skeleton version also not the person apply.sh named, with the attestationAccess Policy → Approval
Apply an approved versionThe host operator./apps/iam/apply.sh <version> --by <username> (since slice 4.5; ./medcea.sh → Policy → Apply an approved policy version). Nothing approved changes anyone's access before then.
Roll back to an earlier versionThe host operator, then a qa person who did not run it./apps/iam/apply.sh --rollback-to <K> --by <username> --reason "<why>", confirmed afterwards in Access Policy → Confirm

Applying, step by step (the host operator, from the repository root, on a committed tree):

  1. ./apps/iam/apply.sh N --check-only --by <you>: every check below, the diff summary and the health baseline; nothing is written.
  2. ./apps/iam/apply.sh N --by <you>: the same checks, then [y/N]; Keycloak and the version are read again after your answer. Then one database transaction applies the version with its grants, and the artefacts follow (gates, route table, network policies, tool settings).
  3. For a skeleton version, check out the approved commit first (the refusal names it), and run ./apps/iam/deploy.sh afterwards: the Access Policy still ships the old skeleton until then.

Refusals you may meet, each with nothing changed (exit 1 unless said):

  • "version N is <status>: nothing to apply" (withdrawn, superseded, applied, draft, submitted); "version N is a <kind> version: apply.sh applies change and skeleton versions; see --rollback-to" (exit 2);
  • "version N was approved on version B, but version A is applied: withdraw it and draft again"; "finish version A first: ./apps/iam/apply.sh --resume";
  • the approval's token: not a realm signature, an unknown key, claims that are not the record's, a token already used for another approval;
  • the approver now: disabled, no approver role any more, another person_id, one of the authors' persons, the person of an enabled admin account (I17); "version N widens role R, which <account> (an enabled account of the approver's person) holds: another qa person approves it";
  • "N account decision(s) wait for qa (the User Manager's Decisions tab): … confirm, refuse or withdraw them first";
  • "the diff of version N is not the one qa approved (… a skeleton or compiler change since the approval alters what it grants): withdraw it and draft again";
  • "these checks fail before the apply (above): fix them first" (exit 2); "apps/iam has uncommitted changes"; "something changed while the confirmation waited (above): … run it again".

If the artefacts cannot be brought to the new version after three attempts, a change or rollback version is reverted to the previous one and the command says so (exit 1); see apply.sh for what a revert does not undo and for --resume.

  • The two switches. Approving and returning go to the database with the qa person's own sign-in token, so they need IAM_POSTGREST_JWT and IAM_DB_ROLES to be true (see IAM switches); otherwise the page says approving is not possible yet. Drafting does not need them.
  • One version in flight. At most one change or skeleton version is open (draft, submitted or approved) at a time. An open change blocks a skeleton proposal and an open skeleton proposal blocks a new draft, until it is applied, withdrawn or returned. A draft also waits while the applied version's last apply has not finished (apply.sh --resume).
  • Returning. A qa person returns a version with a note. A returned change becomes a draft again for its authors; a returned skeleton version is withdrawn, and the host operator proposes again.
  • Withdrawing. Any admin withdraws the open version in the Access Policy (Withdraw…), approved ones included. When the page cannot (a rollback, a broken page), the host operator runs ./apps/iam/apply.sh --withdraw-open --by <username> --reason "<why>".
  • Break-glass blocks approvals. While a policy break-glass is open nothing can be approved, until a normal deploy ends it.

Every draft, saved cell (with the checker's verdict), submission, approval, return and withdrawal is an event in the tamper-evident policy history, with who, when and the reason or note. An approval and a return also keep the signed sign-in token as evidence. Nothing is ever deleted: a withdrawn version stays in the record.

Why can't I open an app

When your role does not admit you to an app, its gate shows a page titled No access to followed by the app's name, for example "No access to Node-RED". The page says:

  • that you are signed in, but your role does not include the app;
  • which roles may open it, by label, as listed in Which web apps each role can open;
  • that you should ask an administrator to change your role if you need it for your work.

It offers Back to the portal and Sign out.

Grafana checks your role in its own sign-in, so it shows no such page: you land on Grafana's sign-in page with a message that the login was denied.

The AI Workbench also checks your role in its own sign-in. Its refusal is the workbench's own error page (403) with the text "No access to AI Workbench", the roles that may open it and the address of the portal. Its File → Log Out, like Sign out in the user menu, ends your platform sign-in.

If you are refused right after switching to another account in the same browser, the previous account's sign-in is usually still active, and the app is checking that account's role. Sign out from the user menu (your name at the top right, then Sign out) and sign in again, or use a private window for the second account. The Sign Out buttons on the portal page sign you out of the portal only, not of the platform: the portal then signs you in again as the first account.

The service portal has its own page, No access yet. It means your account has no platform role at all, which usually means your access request has not been confirmed yet.

A sign-in failure also shows an error page, but with its own message (starting "Login Failed") and a Try again button. That is a problem with the sign-in itself, not with your role.

Being able to open an app does not mean every button works for you. If an action inside an app is refused, the service checked your role for that action. Ask an administrator which role the action needs, or see the role descriptions in The eleven roles.
