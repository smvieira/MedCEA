Getting access and roles

Every person signs in to MedCEA with one personal account, and every account holds exactly one platform role. You ask for an account on the sign-in page. An administrator reviews the request, and a different person holding qa confirms it before the account can be used. Your role decides which web apps you can open and what you may do in them. This chapter covers how to request access, what each of the eleven roles is for, how to sign in and out, how to manage your profile and password, what happens when your role changes or your account is deactivated, and what to do when a page says you have no access.

Requesting access

The platform has one sign-in page, served by Keycloak in the MedCEA look. It has two tabs: Sign in and Request access. Below the sign-in form there is also a link, "No account yet? Request access". Opening https://access.<domain>/register brings you to the same tab.

  1. Open the sign-in page and choose the Request access tab.
  2. Fill in every field and choose the role you need.
  3. Choose Submit request. The page answers "Request received. You will get an e-mail once an administrator has reviewed it."
  4. Wait for the e-mail. Until then, signing in shows "This account is not active".

The form refuses a request when another request for the same e-mail address is already waiting, or when the username or e-mail address already has an account. One network address can send at most 10 valid requests per hour.

What happens to your request

Submitting creates your account at once, but disabled. It stays disabled until two different people have acted on it:

  1. An administrator opens the request in the User Manager under Requests, and either approves it with a role (the one you asked for or a different one) or rejects it with a note explaining why. The administrator also writes the e-mail you will receive, starting from a prepared text, or unticks Send e-mail to (followed by your address) so that none is sent.
  2. An approval does not enable the account. It becomes an account decision that waits under Decisions until a person holding qa confirms or refuses it. That person must not be the administrator who approved it, and must not be you.
  3. When qa confirms, the account is enabled with the granted role and the e-mail the administrator wrote is sent. If the granted role differs from the one you asked for, the prepared text the administrator starts from says the role was reassigned.

A rejection, or a qa refusal of the approval, deletes the disabled account. You may apply again.

E-mails depend on the platform's mail account, which an administrator sets up in the User Manager under E-mail settings. Without it, the decision is still recorded but no e-mail goes out, so ask an administrator directly.

Your request is stored with every field except the password, which only Keycloak keeps. Each step (submitted, approved, reassigned, rejected) is added to the request's history, with who decided and when. The approval and the qa confirmation are kept as one account decision that names the maker, the checker, both times and any notes, and the e-mail text the administrator wrote is stored with the request.

One platform role per account

An account holds exactly one platform role. To do work that needs another role, ask an administrator to change your role; you do not get a second one. When a role changes, the new role is added before the old one is removed, so a failure in between leaves the account with two roles for a moment, never with none.

Two rules protect the separation between administration and approval:

  • No person may hold an admin account and a qa account at the same time. The User Manager checks this against all of a person's accounts, enabled or not, when a role is changed and again when the change is confirmed.
  • The User Manager refuses to take away the last enabled admin or the last enabled qa.

Operators and their units

An operator works only in the units assigned to them: they see the telemetry, runs and run logs of those units and start or stop runs only there. With no unit assigned they see none of this. The administrator assigns units on the platform host with apps/iam/units.sh, not in the User Manager. Each assignment waits until a qa person other than the administrator confirms it in the User Manager under Decisions; a removal takes effect at once. A production_manager is not limited to assigned units. The Administration chapter describes how assignments are made.

Opening a web app is only the first gate. What you may do inside it (create a recipe, start a run, approve a change, export data) is decided again for each action by the service and by the database, from the same policy. For example, every role can open the Configuration UI, but only some roles can save a draft there and only qa can approve one.

The service portal shows every card to everyone. A card for an app your role cannot open leads to the "No access" page described in Why can't I open an app.

Signing in and out

  1. Open any platform address, for example the service portal. If you are not signed in, you are sent to the sign-in page.
  2. On the Sign in tab, enter your Username or e-mail and your Password. Show displays the password as you type it.
  3. Tick Remember me if you want Keycloak to keep your sign-in on this browser after you close it.
  4. Choose Sign in. You return to the page you asked for.

One sign-in covers every web app on the platform: once signed in, opening another app does not ask for your password again. pgAdmin shows a MedCEA sign-in button first; choosing it uses the same sign-in.

Messages you may see on the sign-in page:

  • "Invalid username or password." Check both and try again.
  • "This account is not active." Your request has not been confirmed yet, or your account was deactivated. Contact an administrator.

To sign out, open the user menu and choose Sign out. This ends your sign-in session, not only the page you are on, and returns you to the app you signed out from, which asks you to sign in again.

The user menu and your profile

Signed-in pages (the service portal, the Configuration UI, the Scheduler, the Monitor, Database Management, MQTT Explorer and the User Manager) show your name with your initials at the top right. Choosing it opens the user menu:

  • Profile opens your profile page.
  • Sign out signs you out.

If the profile service cannot be reached, the menu shows "Account" instead of your name and still offers Sign out. In that case Sign out ends your sign-in for that app only, so close the browser if you need to be sure you are signed out everywhere.

Profile

The profile page, "Your MedCEA account", has two parts:

  • Personal details: you can change your first name, last name and organisation and choose Save changes. Your username and e-mail are shown but cannot be changed here; ask an administrator.
  • Password: Change password takes you to the platform's password page. You may be asked to sign in again first. When the change is done you return to your profile, which says "Your password was changed." The password goes only to Keycloak, never through the platform's own services.

The Back to link at the top left (for example "Back to portal") returns you to the page you opened the menu from, or to that app's home page.

Forgotten password

The sign-in page shows a Forgot password? link only when the platform's mail account is configured and confirmed. Then:

  1. Choose Forgot password? on the sign-in page.
  2. Enter your username or e-mail address on the "Reset your password" page.
  3. Follow the link in the e-mail you receive and set a new password.

When the link is missing, mail is not configured: ask an administrator for help.

An administrator sets the mail account in the User Manager under E-mail settings. The change takes effect, and the Forgot password? link appears, only after a qa person confirms it under Decisions. This stops an administrator from sending password-reset mail through a mailbox they read.

When your role changes or your account is deactivated

Only an administrator changes a role, activates or deactivates an account, in the User Manager under Users. A role change and a deactivation need a reason; for an activation the reason is optional. An administrator cannot change their own account.

ChangeWhen it takes effect
Role change that gives more accessWaits under Decisions until a qa person confirms it.
Role change that only removes accessApplied at once. Any MQTT Explorer credentials you hold are revoked.
Activation of a disabled accountAlways waits for a qa confirmation.
DeactivationApplied at once. Every sign-in session is ended, MQTT Explorer credentials are revoked, and any decision still waiting about the account is withdrawn, except unit assignments.

You do not need to sign out for a role change to reach you. The sign-in gate in front of each app checks your session with Keycloak every 4 minutes, and the proof of sign-in passed to the services is valid for 5 minutes. So, for the apps behind such a gate (all except Grafana, the AI Workbench and pgAdmin, which keep their own sessions; the AI Workbench is described below the list):

  • after a role change, the apps you can open and the actions you may take follow the new role within about 4 minutes;
  • after a deactivation, your sessions end at once, and a page you still have open stops working within about 4 minutes, at the next check.

The AI Workbench checks your role each time you sign in to it, and a workbench session lasts at most an hour before the sign-in runs again (without a password while your platform session is open). So a role that no longer includes the workbench, or a deactivation, reaches it within an hour; a notebook tab left open stops working by then too. Your files are kept, and come back if access is restored.

Every role change, activation and deactivation is kept as an account decision, with the reason, who made it, who confirmed it when confirmation is needed, and the times. Each one that takes effect is also added to the account's status history, with the old and new role for a role change. These histories can only be added to, never edited.

Why can't I open an app

When your role does not admit you to an app, its gate shows a page titled No access to followed by the app's name, for example "No access to Node-RED". The page says:

  • that you are signed in, but your role does not include the app;
  • which roles may open it, by label, as listed in Which web apps each role can open;
  • that you should ask an administrator to change your role if you need it for your work.

It offers Back to the portal and Sign out.

Grafana checks your role in its own sign-in, so it shows no such page: you land on Grafana's sign-in page with a message that the login was denied.

The AI Workbench also checks your role in its own sign-in. Its refusal is the workbench's own error page (403) with the text "No access to AI Workbench", the roles that may open it and the address of the portal. It has no sign-out button; sign out from the portal.

The service portal has its own page, No access yet. It means your account has no platform role at all, which usually means your access request has not been confirmed yet.

A sign-in failure also shows an error page, but with its own message (starting "Login Failed") and a Try again button. That is a problem with the sign-in itself, not with your role.

Being able to open an app does not mean every button works for you. If an action inside an app is refused, the service checked your role for that action. Ask an administrator which role the action needs, or see the role descriptions in The eleven roles.
